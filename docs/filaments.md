# Filaments on the K2 + CFS: what works, what doesn't, and how to set up a non-Creality spool

Written 2026-09-10 for the Creality K2 (F021) with one CFS unit. Every rule below carries its
source; "k2ctl" marks behavior of our own software. The catalog that drives k2ctl and the
Creality Print presets lives in `deploy/profiles.json` (generated by
`tools/filaments/gen_filaments.py --export-json`).

## CFS: do

- **Do use rigid 1.75 mm filament of "medium hardness"**: PLA, PETG, PET, ABS, ASA, HIPS,
  PLA-CF, PA-CF, and *dried* PVA/BVOH are the materials Creality lists as compatible.
  [Creality wiki: CFS filament compatibility](https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/cfs-filament-compatibility),
  [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM)
- **Do use 1 kg spools of 197-202 mm diameter and 42-68 mm width**; anything else jams or
  rattles in the bay. [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM),
  [wiki](https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/cfs-filament-compatibility)
- **Do use cardboard spools only if the edges are finished and the spool is not deformed**;
  Creality's own cardboard spools are fine. [wiki](https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/cfs-filament-compatibility)
- **Do seat the spool so it unrolls freely under the spring-loaded batten bar**, and feed
  the filament end into the bay until the roller takes it. [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM)
- **Do let RFID spools identify themselves** (Creality spools carry a tag; hold the tag over
  the scanning area on the side panel). For everything else, set vendor/type/color/temps
  manually: on the touchscreen, in Creality Print's device page, or with k2ctl (bay menu in the
  tray, or the bay card on the dashboard). [Creality wiki: K2 TPU guide](https://wiki.creality.com/en/k2-flagship-series/k2-plus/print-tpu-filament),
  [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM)
- **Do watch the humidity reading and swap the desiccant** when it climbs; the CFS is
  sealed storage with replaceable desiccant, not a heater. Dry hygroscopic materials (PETG,
  PA, TPU, PVA) in a dryer before they go in. [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM),
  [Creality forum: drying with the K2](https://forum.creality.com/t/drying-filament-or-silica-packs-with-k2/30671)
- **Do tune flush volumes when mixing materials or colors**: Creality Print's flush
  multiplier 1-1.3 is the usual range; transparent filaments need more, matte less.
  [Creality wiki: color mixing](https://wiki.creality.com/en/k2-flagship-series/k2-plus/multi-color-mixing-issue)
- **Do keep the materials in one multi-color job within a similar temperature class**
  (PLA with PLA, PETG with PETG). The printer runs one nozzle temperature per filament change
  and the same chamber/bed for the whole job; a PLA + ABS job either warps the PLA or
  under-bonds the ABS. k2ctl's fan recommendations follow the *feeding* bay's material.
- **Do use "same material" auto-refill deliberately**: the printer groups bays with the same
  material id and color and can continue from another bay when one runs out (`box.same_material`,
  `auto_refill` on the printer). Non-Creality spools get material id `0`, so they only group
  by type + color. (k2ctl / printer API observation, see `docs/printer-api.md`.)

## CFS: don't

- **Don't load TPU or any flexible filament in the CFS.** It bends in the PTFE path and causes
  feeding and unloading failures. Print flexibles from the external spool holder.
  [Creality wiki: K2 TPU guide](https://wiki.creality.com/en/k2-flagship-series/k2-plus/print-tpu-filament),
  [wiki: CFS compatibility](https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/cfs-filament-compatibility)
- **Don't use TPU softer than 95A on this printer at all** (90A, 85A, 80A, 75A and so on): the extruder
  gear flattens it and the nozzle clogs. [Creality wiki: K2 TPU guide](https://wiki.creality.com/en/k2-flagship-series/k2-plus/print-tpu-filament)
- **Don't load damp PVA/BVOH**; wet support material is explicitly unsupported and gums up the
  path. Dry it first. [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM)
- **Don't use warped or raw-edged cardboard spools, 250 g/500 g mini spools, or 2-3 kg spools**
  (outside 197-202 mm x 42-68 mm). Respool onto a compliant spool or use the external holder.
  [CFS user manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0DMZSHTTM)
- **Don't expect the CFS to dry filament.** It reports temperature/humidity and holds desiccant;
  drying is a separate dryer (Creality Space Pi or similar, 45-70 °C, 0-48 h).
  [Creality forum](https://forum.creality.com/t/drying-filament-or-silica-packs-with-k2/30671)
- **Don't switch the feed path mid-job.** Moving from CFS feeding to the spool holder (or back)
  is a touchscreen procedure: unload from the CFS, retract, then load from the holder.
  [Creality wiki: from CFS to spool holder](https://wiki.creality.com/en/k2-flagship-series/from-cfs-to-spool-holder)
- **Don't print abrasives with a worn nozzle.** CF/GF/glow/metal-filled filaments are fine in
  the CFS (the K2 ships a hardened steel nozzle) but they wear it; check the nozzle when
  quality drops. [Prusa KB: composites](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/composite-materials-filled-with-carbon-kevlar-or-glass_167387),
  [Bambu Lab PLA-CF](https://bambulab.com/en/filament/pla-cf)
- **Don't change a bay that is feeding a running print.** k2ctl refuses it (HTTP 409), the
  touchscreen greys it out.

## TPU specifically (including the AMOLEN Transparent Rainbow TPU)

The AMOLEN Transparent Rainbow TPU is Shore **95A ± 2**, 220-240 °C nozzle, 30-65 °C bed,
fan on, print **under 60 mm/s**, retraction 0.5-1.0 mm at 20-30 mm/s, dry 55 ± 5 °C for
6-8 h, density 1.19. [AMOLEN product page](https://www.amolen.com/products/tpu-rainbow)

- 95A is the one flexible grade Creality allows on the K2, but **only from the external spool
  holder**, never through the CFS. k2ctl's catalog marks it `cfs.ok = false`; assigning it to
  a CFS bay is refused unless you click "Load anyway".
- Load it on the holder, select it in Creality Print with the
  "AMOLEN Transparent Rainbow TPU 95A @Creality K2 0.4 nozzle" preset (Generic TPU base, 230 °C,
  45 °C bed, 3 mm³/s cap), and keep travel speed modest.
- Its color shifts along the spool; the dashboard and tray show the gradient, the printer's
  external-holder record gets the middle color.

## How to set up a non-Creality spool (our system)

1. **Pick or add a catalog profile.** `tools/filaments/gen_filaments.py` holds the
   table (`add(vendor=…, name=…, base=…, nozzle=…, lo=…, hi=…, bed=…, fan=…, dry=…, warn=…,
   cfs=…, colors=…, src=…)`). Bases are Creality's own K2 presets so speeds, cooling ramps and
   the CFS ramming/purge settings stay tuned for the machine; only the manufacturer numbers are
   overridden. Run it once to write the Creality Print user preset(s) (app closed), and
   `--export-json deploy/profiles.json` to refresh k2ctl's catalog.
2. **Deploy the catalog** with `deploy/deploy.sh` (k2ctl reloads `profiles.json`; the tray and
   dashboard pick it up on the next refresh).
3. **Put the spool in a bay (or on the holder) and assign the profile**: tray > Filament
   slots > bay > vendor > profile, or dashboard > bay card > pick. Rainbow spools take a color
   list; single-color spools take a color picker. The printer's bay record gets vendor, name,
   type, temperatures and pressure advance, so the touchscreen and Creality Print's CFS view show
   the right material and the slicer's auto-mapping matches by type.
4. **Read the cautions**: the dialog shows the profile's drying advice and warnings
   (abrasive, speed limits, softness); a CFS-incompatible profile is refused with the reason.
5. **In Creality Print** choose the matching `@Creality K2 0.4 nozzle` preset for that
   filament slot, then "sync from CFS" or map manually; check flush volumes for multi-color.
6. **Before printing** dry anything hygroscopic per the profile (`dry` field), and use
   k2ctl's "Recommended fans" for the feeding material as the starting point.

## Blocked sources

`us.elegoo.com`, `fiberlogy.com`, `polymaker.com` and `wiki.polymaker.com` are blocked by this
network's DNS filter, so their numbers came from their EU mirrors, retailer copies of the
data sheets, or the search index; Amazon Basics publishes no temperatures at all and was left
out.
